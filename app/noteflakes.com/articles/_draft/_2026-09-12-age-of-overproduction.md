I've noticed that as one grows older, it feels as if time itself is
accelerating. Many people my age and older have shared this sentiment with me,
and I think in a way it's natural to feel that way - after all, at 52 years old,
with each passing day I'm getting closer and closer to the end of my life, so it
should only be natural that time seems to accelerate. But then again, I think
this also has to do with how our societies have changed in the last few decades,
and even more markedly so with the advent of so-called AI technology.

In the last few years, humanity has been on a binge. In spite of the clear
warning signs that we're heading for a climate catastrophe, the turmoil we're
all experiencing as the result of the failure of the Nation-State to provide
solutions for ethnically heterogenous societies, and the pure idiocy and grift
of contemporary leaders (who, unbelievably, dare talk about meritocracy!), it
seems that the current political and social zeitgeist is all about carelessness
and greed - spending money we don't necessarily have on stuff we don't need,
worshipping at the altar of the golden calf, not caring one bit about the
future, and constructing an identity for ourselves made not of culture,
tradition, heritage or even "values", but through consumer choice - the brands
we buy, the TV series we *binge* on, our favorite pop stars and all the other
silly diversions modern capitalism is throwing our way.

Some have been calling this the age of overconsumption: "I consume, therefore I
am." And the message that's being incessantly repeated everywhere is: "Work for
money, spend that money, and shut up". The elites are not interested in your
opinion, only in remaining in power. The connection between capital and
political power is not even hidden anymore, it's just taken as a fact of life in
a decomcracy, as if it's totally OK for rich people to buy the president's
favor, as if it's totally normal for one man to be worth a trillion dollars,
because "he worked very hard for it".

At the same time, those of us who "haven't worked hard enough", or maybe weren't
born to the right parents or maybe just weren't lucky enough, are expected to
idolize the Zuckerbergs and the Musks and the Altmans and the Trumps, to
secretly be envious of them, and at the same time despise them because they made
it and we didn't. We're expected to buy into the religion of capital, and
consume our way out of our misery, because money is the ultimate solution to any
problem we face, it is the panacea of our existence.

In the last few decades we have seen growth rates in the western world come down
to [almost
zero](https://fortune.com/2025/10/07/data-centers-gdp-growth-zero-first-half-2025-jason-furman-harvard-economist/),
we might also be witnessing peak oil production in the coming decades (according
to the IEA conventional crude oil production already peaked around 2007), and we
have also seen the rise of inequality in western societies, and especially the
rise of the ultra-rich. Meanwhile, the earth is warming up due principally to
CO2 emissions, as [predicted already in the 19th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svante_Arrhenius#Greenhouse_effect).

A metaphor I keep thinking about is that of planet Earth's resources as a lemon
being squeezed by humanity. We're busy squeezing the lemon, we've already got
most of the juice out, and now we're squeezing harder (shale oil is a prime
example) for a few more drops of fossil fuels, of metals, and, indirectly,
economic growth. And, as economic growth has dwindled down to a trickle, it
seems that the ultra-rich have been expecting to enlarge their share of the
lemon pie. They want their 10% yield no matter what.

One way to deal with the slower growth has been the idea of generating demand,
which has lead to overconsumption - buying stuff we don't need, programmed
obsolescence, enshittification, even obesity. But it has occurred to me that
with the current AI craze we have been entering a new phase of capitalism, that
of overproduction. The idea is simple: increase supply regardless of actual
demand, in the hope that eventually demand will grow and catch up.

In the field of software development, this theory has proven correct - demand
for AI services is currently exploding, as more and more companies integrate AI
into their software development processes, and ...



In all the online discussions about how AI is transforming software engineering,
we often hear how people are becoming 10x or 30x or 50x more productive, being
able to finish in days what would previously would have taken months. Similarly,
popular open source projects have seen an explosive growth in the number of PRs
(and subsequently the number of changes therein) submitted.
