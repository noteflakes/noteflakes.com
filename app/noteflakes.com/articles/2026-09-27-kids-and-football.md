title: "On Kids and Football"
layout: article
---

My father was born and raised in a village called Moldovița, nestled at the feet
of the densely forested Carpathian mountains in Romania. One of my favorite
tales he used to tell us about his childhood is about the Jewish kids' football
team Maccabi Moldovița. All of the boys dreamed about playing in a football
team, but they didn't even have a real ball. This didn't stop them though, and
they came up with a plan: the Schmoll shoe paste company had a sales promotion:
collect 100 shoe paste caps, send them to the factory, and you'll get a free
football! The kids did their best to waste as much shoe paste as they could,
which of course led to the indignation of their parents, but finally they
achieved their goal and became the proud owners of two new, shiney footballs.

The only thing missing now was a football stadium. The local priest has kindly
donated a piece of land belonging to the Orthodox church to the Jewish football
enterprise, and the fathers improvised two goal frames from some pieces of
lumber, but without nets. In order to inaugurate their new stadium, the families
invited the venerated Maccabi Câmpulung club to a friendly match. If they
didn't lose, so they were promised, they'd be able to join the Maccabi league of
Bukovina!

The long-awaited day arrived, and the kids were waiting for their guests at the
bus station, but the bus didn't show up. A few hours later, the news came that
the bus broke down, but the guests were on their way on a chariot drawn by a
horse. The Moldovița boys ran to the outskirts of the village and sat at the
side of the road, waiting impatiently. When the Maccabi Câmpulung players
finally arrived, there was much rejoicing, and everybody precipitated to the new
stadium, some by chariot, some by foot.

The Moldovița players, who prepared for the occasion for weeks, bravely
withstood the challenge, and the game was still at 0-0 at the 85th minute. My
father, who was a wing back, tried to repel an incoming attack by a Câmpulung
player, and had succeeded in kicking the ball away, but unfortunately he kicked
it straight into the goal, the Moldovița goal! At first he didn't understand
what happened, but after a few seconds he realized the disaster that befell his
team! There's no need to recount the last moments of the game. Maccabi Moldovița
lost, all because of him. As soon as the game ended, he ran home, broke into
bitter tears on his bed, and wept himself to sleep. In the following days he was
filled with shame, and his friends turned their back on him, but with time they
forgave him. That was the end of Maccabi Moldovița's attempt to get into the
Maccabi league, and besides, in a few short years [all of their dreams became
impossible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Bukovina#Soviet_occupation_and_Axis_period).

This all happened almost 90 years ago. Yesterday, September 26th 2026, in the
village of [Umm al-Khair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umm_al-Khair,_Hebron) in
the West Bank, the local children waited impatiently in their football stadium
for their guests, Palestinian national team players as well as a delegation of
Israelis and foreigners, for a friendly match, but the guests did not arrive. In
their stead, a group of soldiers from the "Israeli Defense Forces" came, holding
a closed military area order, and commanded all present to leave immediately.
Why? Because.

My father has left us more than ten years ago, but his stories about the
trampled dreams, children's dreams, about the persecution, the cruelty, the
violence and the life of refugees (which fortunately came to an end a few years
later), still live in us. What would he have said, what would he have done, if
he saw how his own people are trampling the dreams of children? And what can I
say, what can I do?
