# A Manifesto of Political Action

A recent trip to the old country has made things perfectly clear to me: the time
has to come to act decisively against a facist, racist, apartheid regime. This
regime is by its nature and its actions beyond any redemption using normal
democratic or legal means. The regime's DNA is the logic of racism, of
geopolitical bullying and of incessant expansion.

Through its actions, the regime has shown its true colors: daily acts of
violence against innocent people; repeatedly violating the sovereignty of
neighbouring countries; perpetrating war crimes and crimes against humanity in
territories it's been occupying for decades: ethnic cleansing, starvation,
deportation, mass imprisonement, mass killings, apartheid laws.

The deafening silence and complacency of the regime's supporters, including the
overwhelming majority of its privileged citizens and Je**sh communities abroad,
and other occidental apologists, has made any recourse to legal or democratic
means through local or international processes a futile exercise.

The line is drawn, all doubts and fears have melted away. This regime must be
fought against, it must be toppled, it must be removed from the stage of
history.

## 1. Political action must be non-violent

There can be any number of justfications for answering the regime's injustice
with violence, but violence brings with it all the problems inherent to the
politics of power. Power corrupts. In the final analysis, any relationship based
on power is doomed to be violent and unjust, and necessarily devolves into
authoritarian politics. In the spirit of liberty, equality and fraternity, any
political action must be non-violent.

Our fight is against a regime, not against individual people. The people
perpetrating the regime's crimes are not our enemies, they are our brothers and
sisters who've lost their humanity. With our political action we must show them
there's an alternative. This can only be done through non-violence.

## 2. Political Use of One's Privileges

We must take advantage of our status as privileged citizens under the apartheid
regime for political action. We have the privilege of getting in and out of the
country without any restrictions. We have the privilege of circulating freely in
*most* of the territories under the regime's control. We have financial
privileges. We have social and cultural capital.

We must put our privileges, our political, economic, social and cultural capital
to use against the regime. Those of us who work for the regime must act against
the regime through their access and privilege.

## 3. The Different Kinds of Political Action

Political action against the apartheid regime can come in different forms:

### Protective presence

There already exist a large number of different associations and collective that
practice "protective presence", meaning the presence of privileged people around
non-privileged communities, families and individuals who are persecuted by the
regime and its militias. This protective presence consists of being present and
documenting the harrasing and persecution of the non-privileged. It does not
consist of responding with force.

Protective presence is perhaps the most potent form of non-violent resistance to
the apartheid regime. It is a sincere expression of solidarity with the
persecuted, and has historically served to somewhat attenuate the level of
violence practiced by the regime and its militias.

Through documentation and participation, protective presence is the best way to
show the regime's supporters that there's an alternative based on solidarity,
justice and the rejection of racism.

### Financial Support

Another major way to resist the regime is through financial means:

- Donating for the protection of the non-privileged. Many protective presence
  and other organisations receive donations in order to finance protective
  presence activities, fencing and security equipment, other material and
  logistical support for the non-privileged, and legal help.

### Auxiliary Support

- Help for non-privileged people harrased and persecuted by the regime and its
  militias:
  - Logistical help.
  - Legal help.
  - Psychological help.
  - Medical help.
  - Diplomatic help.

- Help for political activists:
  - Psychological help.
  - Legal help.
  - Medical help.
  - Logistical help.

### Clandestine Exfiltration of Information

Political activists embedded in the regime's organisms may act to exfiltrate
information and documentation in order to make the regime's actions public
knowledge, and to help other political activists in their actions.

## 4. The Importance of Discretion

Any political action must be discreet. Even though the regime has not yet
started to *systematically* persecute privileged political activists (except for
a few relatively isolated cases), it may start doing so at any given moment. For
the sake of efficiency, political action must be discreet.

The regime monitors social networks, the regime may spy on political activists,
the regime *is* practicing mass-surveillance on digital communication networks,
and may be surveiling not only non-privileged individuals but also privileged
ones. Therefore discreet means of communication must be developed.

## 5. The Changing of Hearts and Minds

Political action may also consist of changing the hearts of privileged citizens.
Nothing is more important than effecting a change in the hearts and minds of the
privileged youth. They are deeply racist, deeply brain-washed, deeply
indoctrinated, deeply ignorant, but they are the future.

The question of racism is central to the changing of hearts and minds. The
problem of dismantling racism should be approached with psychological and
ideological tools. A method of questioning should be used to show the person
concerned the absurdity of their racism, and provide them with a framework for
breaking out of their "internal ghetto".

A further step is the recruitment of political activists. This must be done
with discretion - it is a given that most of the regime's privileged citizens
will put their loyalty to the regime above all other considerations and may
denounce a political activist to the regime's police or the secret police, even
if it's a family member.
